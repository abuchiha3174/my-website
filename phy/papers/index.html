Mitigating Losses of Superconducting Qubits Strongly Coupled to Defect Modes

D. Colao Zanuz, Q. Ficheux, ... C. Eichler, A. Wallraff, J.-C. Besse — ETH Zurich, arXiv:2407.18746 (2024)

Measures how many TLS defects couple strongly enough to individually damage a qubit, across 92 devices — and shows those defects reshuffle between cooldowns even though their total count stays constant. Directly relevant to whether a suppression pulse designed around one defect map will still work after the next thermal cycle.

Dynamical Suppression of Decoherence in Two-State Quantum Systems

Lorenza Viola, Seth Lloyd — MIT, Phys. Rev. A 58, 2733 (1998)

The foundational paper for dynamical decoupling — shows that flipping a qubit fast enough with control pulses can wash out decoherence entirely, for any noise spectrum. The theoretical ancestor of the whole suppression-pulse idea, with a concrete design condition (pulse spacing vs. noise correlation time) directly relevant to loss-function design.
