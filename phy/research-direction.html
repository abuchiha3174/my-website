▶ PHASE 1 Shared simulatoropen question weeks 1–4

What it is

A validated driven-TLS Hamiltonian (tunneling model + time-dependent drive term) that reproduces the paper's sidebands and dressed-state behavior, plus a labeled library of simulated ring-down traces with known ground-truth parameters. Both branches were meant to consume this exact same library.

Open question

hamiltonian_generator_dataset.py and generate_dataset.py in Fitzpatrick lab's manuscript codebase implement something that looks like this model on paper — but since the generator script was written on a whim and isn't understood yet, that can't be assumed to be a validated foundation. A second, separate question: does the collaborator's TLS-ML-Paper/ direction (real experimental data, unsupervised) replace the need for a simulated ground-truth library at all, for at least part of this project?

Steps (don't assume, verify)

  • Understand hamiltonian_generator_dataset.py well enough to say whether it's trustworthy — or decide it needs rebuilding.
  • Decide, with input from the collaborator conversation, whether Phase 1–2 still center on simulated ground truth, or partly shift toward the real-data approach.
▶ PHASE 2 · primary Branch Anew work wks 5–9

What it is

An inference model mapping a raw ring-down trace (or its phase-FFT spectrum) directly to the driven-TLS parameters — automating the manual, one-at-a-time curve-fitting currently used.

Why this is the primary deliverable

Self-contained, clear success metric (predicted parameter vs. known one), and parallels published work at Siddiqi's AQT lab and Oliver's group (see comparison table below).

Steps

  • Train a regression/inference model on the Phase 1 trace library.
  • Validate against known simulated ground truth.
  • Test the trained model on real experimental BCTDS data (sim-to-real transfer).
  • Benchmark speed and accuracy against manual fitting.

Realistic Dec 1 outcome

A working model with a measured accuracy benchmark against manual fitting.

▶ PHASE 3 · stretch Branch Bnew work wks 5–12

What it is

Designing a pulse sequence — via evolutionary/neuroevolution search — that creates destructive interference in TLS ring-down dynamics near the qubit operating frequency, suppressing TLS-induced decoherence.

Why it's the side project

Higher compute cost, fuzzier success criterion than Branch A, and full validation eventually needs Fitzpatrick's real hardware.

Steps

  • Define the suppression objective as a fitness function against the Phase 1 simulator.
  • Run an evolutionary, gradient-free search over the pulse-shape space.
  • Validate the best-found pulse vs. a no-suppression baseline, in simulation.
By Dec 1

Validated simulator · complete Branch A result with accuracy benchmark · preliminary Branch B search results — still pending the Phase 0/1 questions above being resolved.
